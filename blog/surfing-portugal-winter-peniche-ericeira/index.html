Surfing Portugal in Winter: Peniche, Baleal and Ericeira

By L'équipe Foamies · 15 November 2026

Peniche, Baleal, Ericeira, plus Lisbon and Nazaré: which spot works for the wind and your level, water temperature, wetsuit and getting around for a winter surf trip in Portugal.

For surfing Portugal in winter, Peniche and Baleal make the easiest base: the peninsula has beaches facing several directions, so one side is almost always surfable. Ericeira, further south and closer to Lisbon, has world-class waves, but mostly over reef (a rock bottom), which suits intermediates and experienced surfers better. Expect water around 14 to 16°C and pack a 4/3 wetsuit with booties.

We already covered the big picture in our guide to winter surfing in Europe from November to March. This one goes deeper on Portugal: which spot works in which wind, where to stay for your level and how to get around.

Surfing Portugal in Winter: What to Expect

From November to March, North Atlantic storms send steady swell onto the Portuguese coast. There are waves most days, but they are usually bigger and more powerful than in summer. The upside: lineups (the area where surfers wait for waves) are generally quieter than in peak season.

The water is at its coldest around late February, roughly 14 to 16°C. A 4/3 with 3 mm booties works for most surfers, and a 5/3 makes sense for long sessions or if you feel the cold. Not sure which thickness you need? Check our wetsuit thickness and water temperature guide.

Daylight is short, so every session window counts. Get into the habit of checking swell size, period and wind the night before. If that still feels like guesswork, here is how to read a surf forecast.

Peniche and Baleal Surf Spots: Pick Your Side by the Wind

Peniche is a peninsula joined to the mainland by a long beach, and Baleal is a small village on an island at the north end of that beach. So some spots face north and others face south and west. When the wind is wrong on one side, it is often right on the other.

  • North wind (the usual pattern): head to the south side, meaning the long beach of Baleal Sul and, if you are experienced, Supertubos.
  • South wind (less common): switch to the north side, at Prainha or Gigi, the little bays right next to Baleal.
Spot Wave type Suggested level in winter
Prainha Bay sheltered by Baleal island, sandy bottom Beginners
Gigi North-side bay, lefts and rights Beginners on small days
Baleal Sul Long sandy beach between Baleal and Peniche Beginners and intermediates
Molhe Leste Right off the harbour wall All levels when small, experienced when big
Supertubos Hollow, powerful beach break, mostly lefts Experienced surfers
Praia da Areia Branca Punchy beach break, about 20 min south All levels, except on the biggest days

A "left" or a "right" is the direction the wave peels, seen by the surfer riding it. A beach break is a wave that breaks over a sandy bottom.

Two spots to leave to others: the Consolação reef to the south, for advanced surfers, with a rock entry, and Papoa in front of town, which only breaks on huge swells and is tow-in territory (surfers are towed into the wave by a jet ski). Even on the easy beaches, watch for sand-covered rocks at Gigi, and ask a local surf school where to paddle out.

Ericeira: A Surf Reserve, Mostly Reef

Ericeira became a World Surfing Reserve in October 2011, the first in Europe, thanks to the number of quality waves packed into a few kilometres of coast. Most of those waves break over reef, so as a winter beginner you need to choose carefully:

  • Foz do Lizandro: a river mouth south of town with a sandy bottom and mostly lefts. It is the spot most often recommended for learning, but it gets crowded and there can be a strong current.
  • São Julião: a big sandy beach further south that picks up more swell than anywhere else around. Good for learners on small days, quickly too big in midwinter.
  • Praia do Sul: a more sheltered beach in town with lefts and rights. It is the fallback when the southern beaches are maxed out.
  • Ribeira d'Ilhas: a right-hand reef break north of town that likes a west-northwest swell and a southeast wind. Beginners stay in the whitewater (the foam of waves that have already broken) on the inside, experienced surfers sit at the peak (where the wave starts to break). Often busy, with rocks in the lineup.
  • Coxos: a fast, hollow right over shallow reef covered in sea urchins. Experts only.

Tide makes a big difference here: some beaches work better from low to mid tide, others at high tide.

One more thing: Ericeira has become very crowded, and many newcomers do not know the rules. Before you paddle out at Ribeira d'Ilhas, brush up on surf etiquette and priority rules.

Where to Stay for Your Level

Your level Best base Why
Beginner Baleal Sheltered beaches within walking distance, lots of surf schools, usually one side working
Intermediate Baleal or Ericeira Baleal Sul to progress, Ribeira d'Ilhas on smaller days
Experienced Peniche or Ericeira Supertubos and Molhe Leste on one side, Ericeira's reefs on the other

If you are a true beginner, winter is a good time to have a coach: instructors know which corner of the beach stays your size.

What about Lisbon? You can surf from the city. Carcavelos is about 25 minutes by train from Cais do Sodré, but winter swells often make it powerful, and the lineup is packed. Across the river, Costa da Caparica is a long beach split up by groynes (rock walls running out from the beach), with peaks for every level.

Further south, Sagres has south-facing beaches that stay smaller when a big west swell hits. We cover it in the winter surfing in Europe guide.

Nazaré is for watching. Roughly from October to March, the biggest swells turn Praia do Norte into a tow-in wave for big-wave specialists. The best viewpoint is the São Miguel Arcanjo fort and its lighthouse. Keep well back from the water's edge: waves can surge a long way up the beach.

Getting Around the Coast in Winter

  • Lisbon to Peniche: about 1 hour 30 by bus (Rede Expressos from Sete Rios, Flixbus from Oriente) or about 1 hour 10 by car.
  • Lisbon to Ericeira: about 45 minutes by car from the airport. By bus, Carris Metropolitana lines leave from Campo Grande and take boards in the luggage hold.
  • Lisbon to Carcavelos: the Cascais line train from Cais do Sodré.

A car makes a huge difference in winter: you can switch sides of the peninsula when the wind changes, or drive down to Ericeira when Peniche is too big.

Staying Safe in Portuguese Winter Surf

  • Surf within your level: if the waves look too big from the beach, they are.
  • Avoid surfing alone at a spot you do not know, especially a reef. We explain why in our article on surfing alone and finding a surf buddy. Once you are there, Foamies can help you find other surfers nearby to share sessions with.
  • Ask surf schools and local surfers about currents, rocks and access.
  • Get out before you are exhausted: in 15°C water, fatigue creeps up fast.

FAQ

Can you learn to surf in Portugal in winter?

Yes, especially in Baleal near Peniche, where small bays like Prainha stay sheltered. Book lessons with a local school and pick the calmer days: in winter, a medium swell can already be too big for a beginner.

Peniche or Ericeira: which is better?

If you are a beginner or want the most surfable days, Peniche and Baleal are easier thanks to beaches facing several directions. Ericeira suits solid intermediates and experienced surfers who enjoy reef breaks.

What wetsuit do I need for Portugal in January?

A 4/3 with booties works for most surfers, with water around 14 to 16°C. Go for a 5/3 if you feel the cold or plan long sessions on windy days.

When is the best season to surf in Peniche?

Autumn, from September to November, is often the sweet spot: consistent swell, water that is still mild and fewer people than in summer. Winter brings bigger waves that suit intermediates and experienced surfers. Summer has smaller waves, which makes it the easiest season to learn.

Is November a good time to surf in Peniche?

Yes. November brings the first winter swells, with water around 16 to 17°C. With beaches facing several directions, you can almost always find a side that suits your level: Baleal Sul in a north wind, Prainha or Gigi in a south wind.
