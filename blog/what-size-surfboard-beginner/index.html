What Size Surfboard Should a Beginner Get? (Size Chart)

By L'équipe Foamies · 22 November 2026

Length, volume and weight: how to choose the right beginner surfboard, and why a bigger board means more waves.

For a beginner, the right surfboard is bigger and has more volume than most people expect: usually 7 to 9 feet long, with plenty of float. A board that is too small is the number one reason people struggle to stand up. Here is how to pick the right size for your weight, height and the waves you surf.

Why beginners need a big board

When you start, everything happens before you even stand up: paddling, catching the wave, staying balanced. A long, thick board floats more, paddles faster and forgives small balance mistakes.

On a board that is too short, you sink, paddle hard without moving and the wave rolls under you. That means lots of effort, very few waves and the feeling you are not improving. A bigger board gets you more waves per session, and catching waves is how you learn to surf.

Length, volume, width: what really matters

Three measurements are written on every board. Here is what they change for you:

  • Length (in feet, for example 8'0): the longer the board, the more stable it is and the easier it paddles.
  • Volume (in liters): the most useful number. More liters means more float.
  • Width and thickness (in inches): a wide, thick board is more stable when you pop up.

If you remember one thing, check the volume. Two boards of the same length can have very different volumes.

Surfboard size by weight

The ranges below are common guidelines for an adult beginner in small to medium waves. They vary between brands and shapes, so treat them as a starting point, not a rule.

Your weight Suggested length Approximate volume
Under 60 kg (130 lb) 7'0 to 8'0 50 to 65 liters
60-80 kg (130-175 lb) 8'0 to 8'6 60 to 80 liters
80-95 kg (175-210 lb) 8'6 to 9'0 75 to 90 liters
Over 95 kg (210 lb) 9'0 and up 85 liters and up

A simple rule of thumb many surfers use: as a beginner, aim for at least as many liters as your weight in kilos, or a little more. Height matters too: if you are tall for your weight, go one step longer.

Foam board, mini-mal or longboard?

Three types of boards work well for beginners:

  • Foam board (soft-top): the safest way to start. It hurts less if it hits you, it is stable and usually cheaper. Most surf schools and surf camps use them.
  • Mini-mal (around 7'0 to 8'0): a good balance between stability and maneuverability, and easy to carry.
  • Longboard (9'0 and up): very stable and great in small waves, but heavier and harder to handle in the whitewater (the broken waves near the shore). Because it gets you into waves early, it is worth knowing surf etiquette and the priority rules.

For your first sessions, a soft-top around 8 feet suits most adults. For the rest of your kit, see our wetsuit thickness guide by water temperature.

The waves matter too

Small, mushy summer waves call for more volume: you need to stay on a wave that has little push. In steeper, more powerful waves, a very big board becomes hard to turn and to get through the breaking waves. To know what to expect before you go, learn how to read a surf forecast.

That is why many improving surfers end up with two boards: a big one for small days and a shorter one when conditions get better. But at the start, one board with plenty of volume is all you need.

Rent before you buy

Before you spend money, rent a few sizes from a surf school or shop. You will quickly feel which one gets you the most waves. A good test: if you paddle and keep missing almost every wave, your board is probably too small.

Second-hand boards are a great way to start, too. A beginner board is there to help you learn, not to look good.

When to move to a smaller board

Think about a shorter board once you stand up consistently, choose your waves and start riding along the wave instead of straight to the beach. Go down gradually, a few inches at a time: jumping to a small board too soon often sets you back.

To see your progress, keep a record of each session: the board, the conditions, what worked. In the Foamies app you can log every session with the board you rode and see what works best for you.

FAQ

Is a 7-foot board enough for a beginner?

For a lighter adult, a high-volume 7'0 can work. For most adults it is a bit short at first: a board around 8 feet makes those first waves much easier.

Should I start on a foam board?

It is the safest and most common choice. It is stable, floats well and reduces bumps and bruises when you fall or get hit by your board.

How long should I keep my first board?

There is no set time. Keep it as long as you are improving on it: once you pop up easily and want to start turning, it is time to try something shorter.
